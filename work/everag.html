Ever.Ag · Agtech design system 2022–24

A product suite, acquired one company at a time.

Ever.Ag makes software for the agricultural supply chain: dairy production, transport logistics, livestock markets, brokerage and futures trading, and the back-office systems that pay everyone. Much of that suite arrived through acquisition, and it looked like it. More than ten products, each carrying the interface of the company it used to be.

In 2022 Ever.Ag brought in Foundry to make it one product family. I led design across a multi-year engagement that grew to dozens of projects. Before we could redesign anything, we needed the thing to redesign it with.

The design system came first.

We started with an interface audit across the portfolio and distilled it into a style guide: color, typography, iconography. From there we built a full Figma design system for web and mobile, with components, usage guidance, and templates for the tables, charts, and dashboards these products live on. One source of truth, published two ways: libraries for designers, and a public style guide for everyone else.

A system only earns its keep when other people build with it. So the system shipped with its process: contribution guidelines, a changelog, a shared Figma workspace, and a weekly design sync. Designers from Foundry, Ever.Ag, and a partner agency, spread across three countries, worked as one team on one set of parts.

Following the milk.

Dairy is a relay race: a farm fills the tank, a hauler moves it, a plant receives it, and a co-op pays everyone for their leg. Ever.Ag has a product at each handoff, and the system let us redesign them as one connected experience instead of four separate apps.

Producers got a portal that reads at a glance: production, components, and quality against the market price of milk. Drivers got a field app that walks a pickup in the order it physically happens: producer, weight, seals, samples, done. And the co-op back office got modern search, saved presets, and load tables that turn thousands of deliveries into payroll.

Then the market side.

The products we shaped most were financial: risk management and commodity tools for producers who hedge feed costs and livestock sales. The dashboards put projected profit and loss, hedge coverage, and live futures prices on one screen, built from the same chart and table components as everything else in the suite.

The same foundation carried to mobile. We took a brokerage product built for big, data-dense trading screens and rethought its markets views for the phone: new chart, table, and flow patterns that fed back into the system for the next product to use.

Results

  • One design language now underlies Ever.Ag products from the milk tank to the trading desk, on web and mobile.
  • Designers at three companies in three countries ship through the contribution process, documentation, and changelog we established.
  • The style guide is public: design.ever.ag.
